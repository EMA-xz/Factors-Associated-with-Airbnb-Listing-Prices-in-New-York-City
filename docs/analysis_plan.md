# Analysis plan and documented modeling decisions

## Research questions

1. Do listing prices differ between Manhattan and Brooklyn after adjusting for observed listing characteristics?
2. Do prices differ between Entire home/apt and Private room within each borough after adjustment?
3. Does the room-type association differ by borough?

The current pre-fit model specification and host split are defined in
`config/model_spec.R` and the generated `reports/model_preparation/model_protocol.md`.
The starting interaction model uses capacity, bedrooms, log minimum nights and
an indicator for a minimum stay of at least 30 nights. Rating is excluded from
the core model to avoid requiring complete scores. These are starting functional
forms, subject to documented training-data diagnostics; a log histogram alone
does not validate them. Interpret appropriate coefficient combinations with
Brooklyn and Private room as reference levels.

## Current audit decisions

- Scope uses the two named boroughs and two exact room-type values.
- Unique listing IDs are required; duplicate IDs stop execution for investigation.
- The preliminary price EDA sample requires an observed, finite, positive price.
- No arbitrary cap on the number of records; no price-tail deletion or imputation.
- Core covariate completeness is now required for the primary model sample;
  ratings remain optional and are not in the core model. EDA eligibility is unchanged.
- Missing prices and missing ratings are never converted to zero.
- Zero bedrooms may represent a studio; missing bedrooms are not assumed to be zero.
- Long numeric IDs are imported as strings to avoid precision loss.
- The source snapshot and its actual per-row scrape dates are recorded separately.

## Questions to resolve before final modeling

- Is price coverage different across borough/room-type and other observed characteristics?
- What distinguishes short minimum stays and minimum stays of 30+ nights across groups?
- Do the four groups have overlapping capacity/bedroom ranges sufficient for fair adjusted comparisons?
- Are there unusual property types or quote definitions that require clarification?
- Are extreme observations plausible, and do they materially change the conclusions?
- Are repeated hosts common enough to warrant host-grouped validation and clustered inference?
- Does adding ratings change the sample composition and conclusions?

The audit retains quote fields in the original source but does not substitute
them for missing `price`. The provider's older dictionary does not document all
newer quote fields; investigate them before making any such change.

## EDA findings that affect model design

See the generated EDA report and CSVs for all numerical evidence. Minimum-stay
composition differs across groups; the room-type category Private room also
contains hotel-like property types. The descriptive flag is the case-insensitive
regex `hotel|hostel|resort` applied to the original property-type text. It is not
a legal classification. The current EDA does not exclude these records. Consider
separate minimum-stay comparisons and an explicitly defined residential-scope
sensitivity analysis, rather than assuming that room type identifies housing use.

The missing-price comparisons report observed characteristics within each group.
They cannot prove that selection on unobserved price is absent. Higher guest
capacities have sparse combinations; consult capacity_support.csv and
bedroom_support.csv before interpreting adjusted contrasts outside common support.

## Later validation

The host-level split is now fixed before regression fitting. Keep all learned
preprocessing within training data. Earlier EDA inspected the entire snapshot;
the split is a prospective holdout for subsequent modeling, not pristine external
validation. Compare test MSPR
with training residual MSE on the log-price scale; specify the MSE degrees of
freedom. Do not repeatedly tune against the held-out test set. Inspect residuals,
variance, collinearity, leverage and Cook's distance. Report effect sizes and
confidence intervals alongside tests. Interpret associations, not causal effects.

## Training-model stage

The original `config/model_spec.R` remains the pre-fit record. Scripts 09-10 now
fit those candidates on the saved training hosts and retain both room types.
Ordinary R summaries are saved, while the main uncertainty calculations allow
within-host dependence through host-cluster HC1 covariance. All conditional
comparisons account for the interaction. Primary and property-label sensitivity
analyses use the same model form and inherited train/test split.

After inspecting M2 training residuals, one refinement was recorded in
`config/model_refinement.R`: log capacity replaces linear capacity. Log minimum
nights is also centered within the two stay regimes, an equivalent reparameterization
of those terms. Script 11 checks the equivalence, compares candidates using five
training-host folds, and repeats diagnostics, sensitivity and influence checks.
The refinement was motivated by the training data, so internal CV and inferential
results are exploratory; they do not assess the entire selection process without
optimism. No reserved test errors have been computed.

M3 is a working specification, not a declaration that all assumptions hold.
See `reports/modeling/training_report.md` for calculated results and remaining
limitations. Retain the planned interaction to address the research question;
do not choose the sample or specification to obtain significance. Remaining
group residual patterns, sparse covariate combinations and host concentration
must be addressed or explicitly acknowledged before freezing the final model.
Rating sensitivity is optional future work, not an implemented analysis.
